One device per pair. Only the signer looks at the secret card. The other student looks away until the card is hidden.

No voice. Do not fingerspell the target vocabulary word. In the sentence rounds, fingerspell proper names or a specific item only when the prompt explicitly requires it. Listing and shoulder-shift contrasts begin in Round 3. For sentence rounds, sign the full prompt; the partner interprets it. Vocabulary cards use Unit 4 only. Switch signer every turn. Personal qualities are excluded. (SHIFT) = contrastive body shift; (LIST-2/3/4) = list items on the non-dominant hand; (SS) = shoulder shift for AND.

Points

Vocabulary: 1 point to signer for clear production + 1 point to partner for correct guess. Sentences: 2 points to signer for complete signing + 2 points to interpreter for accurate meaning. +1 structure point to signer for clearly executed listing/contrast where marked. Optional +1 teamwork bonus each for appropriate NMS or successful clarification. No bonus if the structure is omitted.

XP is separate from match points. Earn 10 XP for a successful sign or interpretation, +5 XP for required grammar, +5 XP for teamwork, and +10 XP for a completed round. Each player starts with three one-use power-ups: Double XP, Hint, and Reroll. They do not change the original point rules. Level up every 50 XP.

    Teacher notes

    Eight rounds: five Unit 4-only vocabulary rounds, then three increasingly demanding sentence rounds: early listing and contrastive structure, family stories, and championship sentences combining both. Each round has six vocabulary turns or eight sentence turns, alternating A and B, for 36 total turns. Play all five rounds, or stop after any completed round for shorter classes. Partners manually judge signing; this game does not pretend to recognize ASL through the camera. Power-ups and XP are optional motivational features; they never override the original match scoring.

    The two short vocabulary rounds are limited to Unit 4 family, relationship, and life-stage signs from previously supplied materials; personal qualities are excluded. Check any locally taught variants. Glosses are classroom prompts, not a claim of a single universal ASL translation. (LIST) indicates enumeration using the non-dominant hand; (SHIFT) indicates contrastive role/space shift. The teacher should model both before playing. (SS) marks a shoulder shift used for AND, as requested; (SHIFT) marks contrastive structure. Sentence prompts are classroom gloss cues, not definitive ASL translations.
